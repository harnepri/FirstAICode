# AI Product Manager Portfolio

This repository is the starting point for building a portfolio that demonstrates practical product judgment for AI Product Manager roles. The portfolio website itself has not been built yet; this README and [context.md](context.md) define its direction and setup plan.

## Purpose

Help hiring teams and product leaders quickly understand the problems you can lead, how you decide whether AI is appropriate, how you manage product and model risks, and what outcomes your work produced. The portfolio should be grounded in your real experience and evidence, not generic AI claims.

## Portfolio Setup

1. Read [context.md](context.md) and use it as the working brief when planning or building the portfolio.
2. Gather your actual work history, target roles, and two or three shareable projects.
3. For each case study, document the user problem, evidence, your role, key decisions, AI suitability, risks, measurement, outcomes, and lessons.
4. Remove confidential details and verify every claim. Label prototypes, estimates, and hypotheses as such; do not publish placeholders as facts.
5. Build a small, responsive portfolio with Home, Case Studies, About, and Contact sections after the content is ready.
6. Check mobile layout, keyboard access, contrast, links, and contact details before publishing.

## Case Study Checklist

- What user and business problem mattered, and how was it validated?
- Why was AI appropriate, or why was a simpler solution better?
- What did you personally own, and who did you work with?
- How did you address data, quality, trust, privacy, and failure handling?
- What metrics defined success, and what results were actually measured?
- What did you learn, and what would you do next?

Use evidence you are allowed to share. Do not invent metrics, employers, customer access, outcomes, or credentials. See the editorial and case-study standards in [context.md](context.md).

## Preview Existing Prototypes

Open [Memory Game](memory-game.html) or [Snake](snake-game.html) directly in a modern browser. To serve the repository locally instead, run:

```powershell
python -m http.server 8000
```

Then open `http://localhost:8000/`. These prototypes are plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no package manager or build step. Their scores and preferences are stored locally in the current browser.
